My Path from Fluid Dynamics and CAE to Physics-Informed Machine Learning



When I was a graduate student, I majored in fluid dynamics and performed multiphase flow simulations using OpenFOAM. Now, I work on building parametric surrogate models for structures containing composite materials.



Through this work, I realized how well the strengths of machine learning match the challenge of computationally expensive simulations. Traditional simulations can take hours or even days to run, which is a real constraint on how quickly engineers can explore the design space.



What draws me to this field is that the quality of an ML model depends heavily on engineering judgment: which data to use, how to structure it, and how to interpolate or model the underlying physics. This is not just a data problem; it is a physics problem. Because of my background in fluid dynamics, I can intuitively sense where a purely data-driven model might fail to respect physical laws — and this is exactly the gap that Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) are designed to address.



I am currently studying machine learning and PINNs. My goal is to build fast, physically-grounded surrogate models by combining my fluid dynamics knowledge, my CAE and surrogate modeling experience, and the latest ML techniques.
